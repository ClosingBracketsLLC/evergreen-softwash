Nobody publishes real prices, every quote requires a phone call, and the range on the phone runs from suspiciously cheap to unexplained thousands. Here's the transparent version.

## The short answer

Around greater Puget Sound in 2026, a professional **soft wash of an average two-story home runs $450–$900**. Larger and more complex homes — three stories, extensive gables, mixed materials, waterfront exposure — run **$900–$2,000+**. Roof treatment is its own line: typically **$700–$1,600** for composite roofs, more for tile and cedar.

At Evergreen our published starting prices are [$595 for a house wash and $895 for a roof soft wash](/services/soft-washing/), with every project confirmed as a fixed written quote before work begins.

## What actually drives the price

- **Height and access.** Every story multiplies time and rigging. A walk-around rambler is half the job of a three-story waterfront home with a daylight basement on the water side.
- **Growth severity.** A light annual refresh and a first-clean-in-a-decade are different projects. Heavy lichen and moss demand dwell time, repeat passes, and more chemistry.
- **Materials.** Vinyl is forgiving. Cedar, EIFS/stucco, and historic paint demand gentler solutions, lower pressure, and slower hands.
- **Landscaping.** Dense foundation plantings must be pre-soaked, covered, and rinsed throughout — a real labor line, and the one cheap quotes silently skip.

## What should be included (ask explicitly)

A professional house-wash quote includes all siding plus soffits, fascia, and gutter exteriors; a window rinse; plant and fixture protection; and some form of longevity promise — ours is a [2-year algae-free warranty](/services/soft-washing/). If any of those are "extra," the low price isn't low.

## Red flags in cheap quotes

1. **"We pressure wash houses."** That sentence alone should end the call — high pressure on siding causes the damage described in [our method comparison](/blog/soft-washing-vs-pressure-washing/).
2. **No certificate of insurance offered.** Uninsured wand work on a ladder is your liability, not theirs.
3. **Price-per-hour.** Professionals quote the outcome, not the meter.
4. **No mention of your plants.** The chemistry that kills algae kills hydrangeas too, unmanaged.

## When washing is an investment, not an expense

Two moments make the math unambiguous: **before listing a home** — brokers consistently report washed exteriors photograph and show dramatically better — and **before repainting**, when a proper wash reveals which surfaces actually need paint and which just needed the grime gone. More than one client has deferred a five-figure paint job a full cycle after seeing their real siding color again.
